# Direction selection

These are original compositional defaults, not universal standards or replicas. Sources: S01–S12, S32–S36, S51. See assets/direction-board.svg.

| Direction | Operator / task | Composition | Signature | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A · Records workspace | Operator finding/comparing people, organizations or domain records | Quiet navigation; compact title; saved views; full-width table; inspector | Column alignment and retained view context | Every property shown at once |
| B · Pipeline workspace | Operator moving opportunities, applications or other staged work | Shared shell; pipeline/owner scope; stages with meaningful counts/totals; concise cards; inspector | Stage and next-action visibility | Unusable drag or misleading totals |
| C · Operations workspace | Manager spotting exceptions | Soft canvas; restrained summary; dominant queue; secondary trend/detail | Decisions and overdue work outrank decoration | Wall of KPI cards replacing work |
| D · Support workspace | Agent resolving a queue with history | Queue nav; record list; active conversation/case; optional customer context | Fast transitions and retained context | Too many narrow columns |

## Select from the job

Use A for “which record?” and field comparison; B for “what stage and next action?”; C for “what needs intervention?”; D for “what should I resolve next?”
These are task layouts, not industry restrictions. Recruiting applications can use B; membership renewals and service follow-ups can use C; client requests can use D. Optional invoice work can combine A/C. Do not use B for data with no meaningful stages. Use domain-adapters.md for the object model.

## Keep options comparable

For an agency CRM, keep the same five clients, currency, overdue task, and workflow. Change list versus board, inspector versus full page, or compact versus comfortable density. Do not compare populated and empty screens. Label synthetic data; include a long name and overdue case.

Choose one or two useful distinguishing details: owner picker, urgency treatment, financial summary, filter chips, or activity timeline. Keep identity in typography, accent, terminology, and spacing. Do not invent navigation behavior for originality.

## Density and shell

Compact density suits frequent keyboard work; comfortable density suits occasional and touch use. Change controls, rows, and gaps together, preserving hit areas.
Do not force a sidebar into embedded software or a successful top-navigation product. Collapse optional context before shrinking primary work. A full record route is preferable to three unreadable columns.
